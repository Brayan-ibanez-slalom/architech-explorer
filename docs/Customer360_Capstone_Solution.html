Module Recap

1. What the Course Teaches

Architecture is justified decision-making under constraint — not a choice of trendy tools. The module builds from understanding → application → judgment.

Understand

Architecture = decisions & trade-offs. Functional vs. quality attributes. ASRs and cost of change.

Apply

Identify objectives/constraints. Write measurable QA scenarios. Build a Utility Tree.

Evaluate

Judge if a decision is justified. Spot hidden trade-offs. Facilitate the objectives → decisions chain.

Key Takeaways

  • Start with the problem space, not the solution.
  • Quality attributes only matter when they are specific and measurable.
  • Not every requirement is architecturally significant — ASRs narrow the design space.
  • The harder a decision is to reverse, the more deliberate it must be.
  • Architecture must evolve: design upfront for critical capabilities, allow details to evolve, preserve optionality, reassess continuously.

3. Customer 360 — Business Context

A retail company wants a Customer 360 capability combining customer data across systems, to support analytics, personalization, service, and future Data Science.

Fragmentation Sources

  • E-commerce orders
  • Loyalty program activity
  • Customer support tickets
  • Marketing interactions

Current Scale

  • 20M customers
  • 5M interactions/day
  • 4 initial source systems
  • 3 years of historical data
  • 3× interaction growth expected within 2 years

5. Functional & Quality Requirements

Functional (what it must do)

  • Ingest data from multiple source systems
  • Validate & standardize incoming data
  • Resolve customer identity across systems
  • Preserve historical customer information
  • Expose trusted data to analytics/DS & apps
  • Protect sensitive information
  • Support new sources/attributes over time

Quality Attributes (how well)

  • Timeliness: dashboards ready by 7:00 AM
  • Latency (tiered): Marketing = hours; Service ≤ 15 min
  • Scalability: absorb 3× growth in 2 years
  • Modifiability: 2–4 new sources/yr, standard onboarding ≤ 2 weeks
  • Security: auditable, controlled PII access
  • Cost efficiency: observable, non-linear cost growth

Step 5

9. Architecture Decisions & Trade-offs

Decision 1 — Hybrid Ingestion Architecture

Use micro-batch/streaming for Customer Service-facing events (≤15 min SLA) and standard batch for marketing/dashboard data (7 AM SLA).

Trade-off: Higher operational complexity (two pipelines) vs. cheaply meeting divergent latency SLAs — avoids over-engineering everything to real-time.

Decision 2 — Dedicated Identity Resolution Service

Build a decoupled golden-record / identity-resolution layer with survivorship rules, separate from source ingestion.

Trade-off: Upfront investment in matching logic vs. long-term trust and reduced rework; near-irreversible once adopted, so worth designing early.

Decision 3 — Standard Connector Framework with Centralized Access Control

Schema-on-write connector standard plus centralized, attribute-level access control & audit logging (not bolted on per source).

Trade-off: More upfront design cost vs. hitting the ≤2-week onboarding SLA and consistent PII governance as sources scale.

11. Cost of Change — Where Do These Decisions Sit?

The harder a decision is to reverse, the more intentional we should be about making it.

Reversible Partially Reversible Near-Irreversible
Dashboard layout, report formats
Choice of streaming tool for speed layer
Connector framework standard (Decision 3)
Identity resolution model / golden record schema (Decision 2)

Decision 2 (Identity Resolution) sits closest to near-irreversible — once source systems and downstream consumers depend on a given golden-record model, changing the matching/survivorship logic requires reprocessing history and re-validating trust. This justifies investing in it early and getting it right before scaling to more sources.

Do Not Invent Requirements

12. Open Questions for the Stakeholder

  • What is the required data retention policy beyond the existing 3 years of history?
  • Is there a specific compliance regime (GDPR, CCPA, etc.) governing PII beyond "controlled and auditable"?
  • What is the acceptable identity-match accuracy / false-merge tolerance for customer resolution?
  • Is there a target/cap on infrastructure cost (e.g., $/customer/month), or is "observability" the only requirement?
  • Do Data Science workloads have their own latency/freshness SLA, or is daily batch acceptable?
